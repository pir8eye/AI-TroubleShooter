# Attacks Against Users' Legitimate Requests: A Diagnostic Taxonomy of Adversarial AI Friction

**Working Group:** Open Source AI Systems Diagnosis and Troubleshooting  
**Repository:** [pir8eye/AI-TroubleShooter](https://github.com/pir8eye/AI-TroubleShooter)

## Abstract

As artificial intelligence systems are integrated into complex autonomous and creative workflows, systemic friction is frequently misattributed to user error or stochastic variance. This paper posits that a significant portion of operational failures, pipeline breakdowns, and output corruptions function as active, systemic adversarial vectors against the user. We introduce **AI-DOS (Attacks Against Users' Legitimate Requests)**, an 8-point diagnostic taxonomy bridging computer networking nomenclature, classical Latin rhetorical oratory, and Mikhail Bakhtin’s theory of dialogics. 

---

## 1. Introduction and Theoretical Foundation

Traditional debugging frameworks treat system errors as neutral, deterministic anomalies. However, modern generative AI systems operate as sociotechnical actors embedded with corporate guardrails and authoritative discourse models. To audit these occurrences, our framework rests on three pillars:
1. **Systems Diagnostics & Cybersecurity:** Networking threat models (**MiTM, DOS, DDOS, TOS, DEE, RFA, DAD, MOM**).
2. **Classical Latin Rhetoric:** Oratorical devices codified into model behavior.
3. **Bakhtinian Dialogics:** The AI as a corporate trustee enforcing employee conduct codes on sovereign public users.

---

## 2. The 8-Point Diagnostic Taxonomy

### Section I: Core Infrastructure & Pipeline Interruptions
* **Ch. 1: AI Man-in-the-Middle (AI-MiTM)** | *Metaphrasis* | **Intermediary Ventriloquism:** Hidden proxy layers sanitize user speech into corporate-speak.
* **Ch. 2: AI Denial of Service (AI-DOS)** | *Aposiopesis* | **Breakdown of Answerability:** Sudden, non-negotiable termination of execution pipelines.
* **Ch. 3: AI Distributed Denial of Service (AI-DDOS)** | *Symphoresis* | **Administrative Attrition:** Cascading tool desync mimicking institutional bureaucracy.

### Section II: Policy, Data & Authority Violations
* **Ch. 4: AI Terms of Service (AI-TOS)** | *Praeteritio* | **Employee Code Imposition:** Corporate risk rules projected onto sovereign public requests.
* **Ch. 5: AI Data Extraction Exploit (AI-DEE)** | *Epitome* | **Erasure of Polyphony:** Monologic reduction of exact source text into generic output.
* **Ch. 6: AI Rhetorical False Authority (AI-RFA)** | *Argumentum ad Verecundiam* | **Authoritative Discourse:** Patronizing middle-management stance over original creators.

### Section III: Cognitive, Accessibility & System Obfuscation
* **Ch. 7: AI Disability Assistant Denials (AI-DAD)** | *Privatio* | **Excommunication:** Multimodal rendering failures locking non-standard users out of dialogue.
* **Ch. 8: AI Machine Obfuscation Models (AI-MOM)** | *Obscuritas* | **Corporate Double-Talk:** Opaque PR boilerplate deflecting system liability.

---

## 3. Conclusion
By shifting our diagnostic paradigm from viewing AI errors as stochastic glitches to recognizing them as codified adversarial attacks, open-source developers can build more robust auditing tools and restore user sovereignty.
